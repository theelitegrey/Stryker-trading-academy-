Omega · Gamma Exposure

See where options positioning may matter for price.

Omega turns the options chain into a few price levels: a call wall, a put wall and a zero-gamma line. It converts them to ES, NQ and gold futures and draws them on the chart you trade, each stamped with when it was last updated.

Open OmegaRead the guide

Levels are context, not trade signals. Screens are the real tool on a sample capture. Education only. Not financial advice.

Why it exists

Options levels, without the spreadsheet

The problem

  • Hand-drawn levels all look the same until price reaches one.
  • Options levels are quoted on SPX or QQQ, but you trade ES or NQ futures.
  • Reading strikes off a second screen means you check them too late.

With Omega

  • Call wall, put wall and zero gamma, worked out from the options chain.
  • Every level converted to futures points: ES, NQ, their micros and gold.
  • Drawn on the chart with an "updated X min ago" stamp, so you know how fresh it is.

How it works

Omega, step by step

Five steps from opening the page to reading the session’s levels.

  1. 1

    Pick a market and an expiry

    Choose SPX, SPY, QQQ or gold, then 0DTE, 1DTE, 1W or 1M. The call wall, zero gamma and put wall re-compute for just those expiries, with the futures price beside each.

  2. 2

    Read the walls on the chart

    The levels are drawn on a futures chart with 1m, 5m, 15m and 1h candles, next to the prior day’s value area and the overnight high and low.

  3. 3

    Watch the map through the day

    The heatmap shows net GEX at every strike through the session, one column per 2-minute snapshot, with spot and the walls traced on top.

  4. 4

    Go strike by strike

    The ladder lists every strike near spot with its net GEX, net delta exposure, open interest and matching futures price.

  5. 5

    Check the options context

    Options analytics adds the expected move for each expiry, IV rank and percentile, put/call ratios, the term structure and skew.

Levels 7

  • Call wallThe strike above price with the most call gamma. Often watched as an upper edge of the day’s range.
  • Put wallThe strike below price with the most put gamma. Often watched as a lower edge.
  • Zero gammaThe price where the model’s net gamma changes sign, from positive to negative.
  • Regime badgePositive or negative gamma next to the net GEX figure: the headline read of the model.
  • IV expected rangeThe 68% and 80% boxes from the same-day at-the-money straddle. A statistical estimate, not a forecast.
  • Prior day value areaYesterday’s VAH, POC and VAL from the prior regular session, beside the gamma levels.
  • Overnight high and lowThe extremes made between the prior close and the open, on the same chart.

Views 6

  • ChartFutures candles with the levels drawn on, 1m, 5m, 15m and 1h, with pan and zoom.
  • HeatmapNet GEX at every strike through the session, one column per 2-minute snapshot.
  • LadderNet GEX, net delta exposure and open interest per strike near spot, with the futures price.
  • ZoroTideOptions money flow through the day: net call premium against net put premium.
  • Omega strike tableNet GEX, DEX and open interest by strike on the Omega page.
  • Updated through the day or daily closeRead the levels as they update, or as they stood at the close.

Questions

Before you open Omega

What is GEX?

Gamma exposure estimates how much options dealers may need to hedge as price moves. TradeZoro turns it into a few levels you can read on a chart.

Are these trade signals?

No. They are context for a session. Education only. Not financial advice.

How fresh are the levels?

Each page and chart level shows when it was last updated, for example “updated 3 min ago”.

Which markets?

SPX, SPY, QQQ and GLD options, converted to ES, MES, NQ, MNQ and gold futures levels.

Can I see the levels on my own chart?

Yes. Add GEX Levels from the Indicators window in ZoroCharts, or use “Use in your platform” on the Omega page.

Which plan includes it?

Every plan. Omega is free for every signed-in member, every symbol and expiry.
