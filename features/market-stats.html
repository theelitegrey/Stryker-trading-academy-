Market Stats · Historical frequencies

See how often it actually happened.

Market Stats counts what past sessions did: how often an opening gap filled, which side of the opening range broke first, how often yesterday’s high or low was reached. Every number shows its sample size and a 95% interval.

Open Market StatsRead the guide

Historical frequencies, not predictions. Screens are the real tool on sample data. Education only. Not financial advice.

Why it exists

Rules of thumb, with the count behind them

The problem

  • “Gaps usually fill” gets repeated everywhere, with no count behind it.
  • A percentage with no sample size could rest on ten sessions.
  • Checking it yourself means exporting bars into a spreadsheet.

With Market Stats

  • 12 reports ask the same questions across up to three years of sessions.
  • Every figure carries N, a 95% interval and a check that both halves of the history agree.
  • Under 30 sessions the card says low sample; under 10 the number is withheld.

How it works

Market Stats, step by step

Four steps from picking a market to checking the sessions behind a number.

  1. 1

    Pick a market and a report

    Choose NQ, ES, SPX, YM, RTY, GC or CL, then a report. Narrow it by date range, weekday and gap size.

  2. 2

    Read the number with its sample

    Each card shows the percentage, its 95% interval, N, the yes count, the typical time and whether the two halves of the history agree.

  3. 3

    Open the sessions behind it

    “Sessions behind this number” lists every session that counted, with its date and value, so you can check the count yourself.

  4. 4

    Check today against history

    The Today panel shows where the current session stands on the gap, opening ranges, initial balance and prior-day range, beside the history for the same setup.

What you can do with Market Stats

Every screen is the real tool on sample data.

Prior day levels

How often the session reached yesterday’s high, low and close, and how long it took.

Opening candle

How often the session ended on the same side as its first 5-minute, 15-minute or 1-hour candle.

The first hour’s range: how often the high, the low, both or neither broke afterwards.

Tap any number to see the dates behind it, filtered to the sessions that did or did not.

Up and down gaps, filled in the first 30 minutes or the first hour, from XS to XL gap sizes.

Questions

Before you open Market Stats

Does it tell me what will happen next?

No. It counts what happened in past sessions. Education only. Not financial advice.

Which markets and how much history?

NQ, ES, SPX, YM, RTY, GC and CL, regular session, up to three years of history. It refreshes once a day after the close.

What does N mean?

N is the number of sessions behind a figure. Under 30 the card is marked low sample; under 10 the number is withheld.

What is the 95% interval?

The range the true frequency likely sits in, given the sample. A wide interval means fewer sessions or a less steady pattern.
