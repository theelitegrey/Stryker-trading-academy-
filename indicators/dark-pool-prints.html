Dark Pool Prints

TZTradeZoro TradeZoroFree Updated Nov 4, 2026
Add to chart

Draws large off-exchange stock trades (dark pool prints) at the exact price and time they printed, sized by dollar value, with $10M+ blocks highlighted.

In plain English

What it shows

  • Large stock trades reported off the public exchanges ("dark pool" or off-exchange trades), drawn at the price and time they printed. Dot size follows dollar value, and $10M or more is highlighted.
  • The chart shows when the list was last updated.

How traders use it

  • See where big blocks traded relative to your levels and recent candles.
  • Open the Dark pool list for the time, price and size of each print.

What it does not tell you

  • A print does not say whether the buyer or the seller started the trade.
  • The list is updated every 2 hours on US trading days. Only US stocks and ETFs on a set list are covered.

Overview

Dark Pool Prints puts each large trade that was reported off the exchanges on your stock chart, at the price and time it happened. Each print is a dot sized by its dollar value, with a label such as "$40M". Prints of $10M or more are filled in mint so the big blocks stand out.

Open the Dark pool list from the chart's right-hand bar (on a phone, the Dark pool button) to see the prints as a table: time, price, shares and value, with a second line for the estimated side and the type of print. Tap a row and the chart moves to that print and rings it.

How it works

US trades done away from the exchanges are reported to a regulator-run trade reporting facility, and each report carries that venue. TradeZoro reads the trade reports for the session, keeps the ones from those facilities worth $1M or more (price times shares), and stores the last five sessions. Each dot is one trade, not a total.

Some reports are not at the market price of that moment: average-price trades, contingent trades, derivatively priced trades and late reports. They are kept in the list with a star, and hidden from the chart unless you turn on "Show average-price / contingent prints". The size setting filters prints at $1M, $5M, $10M or $25M.

Hover or tap a print for more detail: an estimated side (Likely buy, Likely sell or Unclear), the type of print spelled out from its sale conditions (regular block, average-price, derivatively priced, late report, after hours and so on), its share of the day's volume, its size against a typical print of that stock, its rank that day, and how far it was from the day's VWAP. Off-exchange reports do not say who bought or sold, or what order type was used, so the side is only an estimate: a print at or above the exchange high of the 5 seconds before it, and above their average price, is a likely buy; at or below the low, a likely sell; anything else is unclear.

Levels: when several big prints trade at the same price on the same day and add up to $25M or more (you can pick $10M to $100M), a dashed line is drawn at that price, labelled with the total and the number of prints.

How to use it

  • Some traders watch where big blocks traded relative to the day's range.
  • Use it to see whether large prints clustered near a level that price later came back to.
  • Some traders compare the prints with volume or VWAP on the same chart.
  • Use the $10M or $25M filter to keep only the largest blocks in view.

These are ways some traders read it, for learning. They are not trade signals.

Notes and limits

  • US stocks and ETFs only, on a set list of 29 symbols including SPY, QQQ, IWM, DIA, AAPL, NVDA, MSFT and TSLA. Futures, crypto and forex show a short note instead.
  • Updated every 2 hours on US trading days; the chart shows when it was last updated (for example "updated 12 min ago").
  • A dark pool print shows that a trade happened at that price. The report does not say who bought or sold, or why; the Likely buy / Likely sell label is an estimate and most prints stay Unclear.
  • Nothing is drawn in bar replay.
